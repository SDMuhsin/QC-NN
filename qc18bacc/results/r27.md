## 3. Lever verdict
The data points at the repair *procedure*, not at max transition, RTL bus locality or placement. Of the 851.8 k um2
the place step adds, 317 k (37 %) is resizing of 74.5 k short (< 0.2 mm, ~1.3-sink) nets, mostly AOI22 mux cells, which
only violate because the long select / broadcast nets feeding them are not yet buffered when repair_design collects its
violators. Repairing long nets first removes 216.5 k um2 (-25.4 %, 12 % of the placed std area) at equal pin count and
equal violator counts. Raising max transition to 0.6 ns gives less than half that (-91 k, -10.7 %) and changes pins by
-1.7 %. The buffers themselves (52 k, 535 k um2, 93 % BUFFD16) are bus-driven: 53.8 % of the added area sits on nets
> 1 mm, led by S-read s_mq (94.5 k, median driver-to-sink 1.86 mm, 62 % across a macro row), s_q ports 65 k, prm 53 k,
m_q 39 k, s_wd 37 k, b_q 29 k. That is the RTL / floorplan locality lever, worth at most ~0.3 mm2. But all repair adds
only +105 k signal pins (+11.5 % over the 914 k pre-repair pins), so no repair_design setting can remove the
pin-density DR stall (R25). Repair-order cuts std area, and so density, by 0.22 mm2; pins have to come from the
netlist. [R] Next: route-free density check of r27_two vs r04 (pins per free um2), then CTS + post-CTS repair
on r27_two (does post-CTS repair claw the area back?).
